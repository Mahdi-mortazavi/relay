Relay shares your Android phone's internet connection with a Windows PC — over
Wi-Fi, the phone's hotspot, or a USB cable.

IMPORTANT: this app is one half of a pair. It does nothing on its own. The PC
needs the free Relay companion app, which you can get from the project's
releases page. Both are built from the same source.

Tap Start Sharing on the phone, click your phone in the list on the PC, tap
Allow. That is the whole setup — there is no account to create, no server in
the middle, and nothing to configure.

WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT

Everything on the PC goes through the tunnel, not only the browser. The link is
WireGuard, so calls, games and downloads work, not just web pages.

Nothing leaves your two devices. No accounts, no analytics, no telemetry, no
crash reporting. The app contains no advertising or tracking libraries of any
kind. The diagnostic log stays on the phone until you choose to share it.

No root is needed, and no VPN profile is installed on the phone.

The keys are made fresh for each pairing and destroyed when you stop sharing.
They are never broadcast: the phone announces only enough to be found, and the
configuration travels over a short exchange that you approve on the phone, with
the requesting computer's address shown to you first.

IF YOU USE A VPN ON THE PHONE

An Android VPN captures apps by identity, which stops Relay answering your PC.
Relay detects this and explains the options. If your VPN app offers a local
SOCKS5 proxy port, Relay can send the PC's traffic through it — so the PC gets
the VPN too, rather than only the phone's own connection. Relay will look for
that port for you.

UPDATES

If you installed Relay from an app repository, that repository handles updates
and Relay will not offer its own. It detects how it was installed and stays out
of the way.

If you downloaded the APK yourself, Relay checks the project's GitHub releases
page and shows a banner when a newer version exists. Downloading and installing
is never automatic — only if you tap the button, and Android asks you to
confirm.

PERMISSIONS

Internet, network and Wi-Fi state: to find the PC and carry its traffic.
Foreground service and wake lock: so sharing survives the screen going off.
Notifications: to show that sharing is running and let you stop it.
Install packages: only for the optional in-app update described above.
